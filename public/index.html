How to read these fast-decision experiments
Each case asks for a bounded choice and separate evidence checks. Test whether the supplied evidence is sufficient for that specific question: text alone, an image alone, complementary sources, or clarification when information conflicts or is missing.
The comparison prompt describes an app-authored teaching hypothesis, not a measured result or an instruction sent to Zoom. “System 1” is a lens for the demo, not a claim about the model’s internal reasoning. Higher probability and faster response time do not establish correctness. Preset inputs stay fixed so comparisons use the same evidence.
What is returned by Zoom, and what does this app calculate?
The app asks Zoom for a choice and its probability distribution, plus two independent predicate probabilities. Questions and choices depend on the selected scenario. The app maps choice values to display labels and calculates probability outside the selected choice as 1 − the selected-choice probability. These probabilities are not guarantees of accuracy.
“What this route means” is a fixed app-authored description, not model-generated reasoning. Evidence lists the supplied sources and independently requested predicates; it is not a citation or a causal explanation. Server latency covers the full Zoom HTTP request and response, including network time.
Questions are independent. The app does not use the predicate answers to claim that they caused the route. Refusals are shown explicitly. Choice and score answers can omit the confidence field; decision rules should use the returned probabilities. This app uses the selected choice’s probability from the returned distribution and does not require confidence. Any confidence field returned remains available in the raw response.
